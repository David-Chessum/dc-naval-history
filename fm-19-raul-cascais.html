FM 19 / Raúl Cascais

When the victorious allies allocated the ex-enemy fleets after World War 1, three small navies, Greece, Portugal and Romania, which had suffered the loss of a minor war vessel during World War One were allocated an equivalent ex-enemy ship in direct compensation for their loss. To compensate for the minesweeping trawler Augusto de Castilho which had been sunk by U139 on 14 Oct 1918 between Madeira and the Azores, Portugal was therefore allocated the Turkish torpedo gunboat Peyk I Sevket. Due to the Turkish revolution the Treaty of Sèvres did not come into effect, and the gunboat was never delivered.

The FM 1 class minesweepers were 170-ton, 141’, shallow-draft minesweepers built by Germany late in World War 1. FM 19 was commissioned on 14 March 1918, and used for submarine training. In 1922 she was transferred to Portugal, where she was commissioned on 26 October 1922 as the motor ferry Raúl Cascais, before becoming a fisheries protection boat on 13 October 1924.

Gröner has claimed that FM 19 was transferred to Portugal as part of war reparations, and this claim has been repeated in numerous secondary sources. It has not been possible to positively link this vessel to the allocation of ex-enemy warships after World War 1, however the fact that Raúl Cascais was a sailor killed on 26 July 1917 when the Portuguese minesweeper Roberto Ivens struck a German mine is suggestive of a possible connection.

Note that Raúl Cascais is often spelt as Raoul Cascaes in English-language sources.
