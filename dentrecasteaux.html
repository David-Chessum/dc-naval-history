D’Entrecasteaux

D’Entrecasteaux was a protected cruiser completed for the French Navy in 1898. After service in World War 1, the obsolete cruiser was employed as a training ship for signalmen, shipwrights and reserve officers at Brest from 1919-1921.

D’Entrecasteaux was stricken on 27 October 1922 in preparation for loan to Belgium. She was towed to Bruges on 24 May 1923, and handed to Belgium on 30 May 1923 on loan as a floating barracks ship for the flotilla of small German A-class torpedo boats left in Belgium by Germany. The French justified this transfer as being outside the restrictions of the Washington Naval Treaty on the basis that this vessel had been disarmed and was immobile and therefore had been deprived of its character as a combat ship, and this interpretation was accepted by default by Great Britain.

The Belgians disbanded their flotilla in June 1926, and returned D’Entrecasteaux, which was towed back to Cherbourg on 4 February 1927 after it was found to be uneconomical to sell her at Bruges.

In 1927 France proposed to transfer D’Entrecasteaux to Poland for use as a training ship. The Poles agreed to pay 1,622,745 francs for the vessel, its fittings, and towing it to Gdynia.

After deciding to sell D’Entrecasteaux, the French, to abide by the WNT, notified the other signatories. Once again Britain did not object to the transfer, on the grounds that they could not now do so, having failed to object to the transfer to Belgium. A Foreign Office official remarked: ‘if there’s any question about this the US can raise it.’

The Poles bought D’Entrecasteaux on 7 Mar 1927. She was commissioned on 30 Jul 1927, reportedly as Król Władysław IV, towed to Gdynia from 31 Jul - 11 Aug 1927, and renamed Bałtyk on 13 Aug 1927. The Polish Navy used her as a stationary training ship, and harbour flagship of the Fleet Commander. In 1933 the ship was hulked and permanently moored at Oksywie naval base (near Gdynia), with concrete blocks placed under her keel to support her. The hulk was captured by the Germans on 19 September 1939, and broken up by them in 1941-42.
