Documents of the Preparatory Commission for the Disarmament Conference

On 25 September 1925 the Sixth Assembly of the League of Nations approved a proposal to initiate preparatory work for a general and all-inclusive World Disarmament Conference. On 12 December 1925 the Council of the League of Nations therefore created a Preparatory Commission consisting of the ten states on the Council, six other European countries (Bulgaria, Finland, Netherlands, Poland, Roumania, and Yugoslavia), and three key non-League members: Germany, the Soviet Union and the US. Argentina, Chile, Greece and Turkey were subsequently invited to join the Commission in 1926-28.

The Commission held six sessions, two in 1926, two in 1927, one in 1928, and the sixth which ran from 15 April - 6 May 1929, when it was suspended to allow the major naval powers to resolve their differences (which did not occur until LNC30). The sixth session resumed on 6 November 1930. The Preparatory Disarmament Commission finally adopted a draft Disarmament Convention for submission to the General Conference on 9 December 1930, ending five years of work.
