Argentine procurement of Churruca class destroyers

In 1927 Argentine initiated a naval construction program which eventually included the construction of two heavy cruisers in Italy, three destroyer leaders in Britain, and three submarines in Italy. In order to fund part of this program, Argentina negotiated a 100,000,000 peso loan with Spain, of which 33,000,000 pesos was spent purchasing two Churrucca class destroyers nearing completion for the Spanish Navy.

The purchase of these two destroyers is said to have had its origin with the flight to South America of the Spanish aeroplane Plus Ultra in 1926. After the plane was gifted to Argentina, the aviators were returned to Spain by the Argentine cruiser Bueno Aires, which proceeded to Cartagena where its officers were favourably impressed with the destroyers then being constructed there for Spain.

The Churruca class destroyers were based on the British Scott class flotilla leaders of World War I, and were built at the Sociedad Espanola de Construccion Naval shipyard in Cartagena, under the supervision of the British shipbuilding firm of Vickers. Churruca and Alcala Galiano had been laid down in 1923 as the first two vessels of the class, and launched in 1925. By the time their sale was agreed they were largely complete, and Churruca had already commenced sea trials.

The loan to Argentina was granted by Royal decree on 24 May 1927, the Argentine crews arrived in Cartagena on 4 November 1927, Churruca was handed over on 7 November to become ARA Cervantes, and Alcalá Galiano on 25 November to become ARA Juan de Garay.

The funds received from the sale were used by Spain to construct two replacement destroyers of the same names, which were laid down in 1929.
