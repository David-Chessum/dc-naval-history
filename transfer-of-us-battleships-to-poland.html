Transfer of US Battleships to Poland

On 5 July 1922, Senator Joseph France introduced a Bill (S. 3787) to the US Senate “to authorize the President, if he shall deem it consistent with the public welfare and the terms of the treaty obligations of the United States, to donate to Poland six of such ships of our Navy as under the terms of the treaties with Great Britain, France, and Japan will no longer be needed by the United States and are listed to be disposed of. By unanimous consent, this Bill was referred to the Committee on Naval Affairs.

This proposal has been exhaustively examined by Bartelski and Bartelski from Polish primary sources, and the following is a paraphrased summary of their findings:

  • The proposal came as a surprise to the Poles, who only became aware of the offer after it was submitted to Congress.
  • The Polish Naval Command recommended accepting the offer for three reasons:
    • The Disarmament Commission in Paris was proposing to limit naval forces such that newly constructed vessels would only be allowed to replace old vessels of equal fighting value, while all states would undertake, until 1930, not to sell, donate or build for other states ships exceeding 10,000 tons. If this provision were approved at the next meeting scheduled for 4 September 1922, the Polish Navy would lose the opportunity to build vessels exceeding 10,000 tons displacement.
    • National defence required the construction of a chain of fortresses along the maritime corridor to prevent an enemy invasion. Using the donated vessels as floating batteries would avoid the expense of constructing very costly fortifications.
    • From a naval perspective these vessels would be useful for training personnel in peacetime, while in wartime they would constitute a substantial fighting force.
  • At the same time, it was noted that taking over even two such ships would require the Navy’s budget and personnel establishment to be more than doubled.
  • Since bringing over all six ships and maintaining them would be too difficult and costly for Poland, it was proposed that two ships each should be donated to Yugoslavia and Romania, in return for certain political, military or economic concessions and advantages.
  • On 14 July 1922, the Council of Ministers resolved to accept the possible donation of the ships.
  • On 19 July 1922, the Polish chargé d’affaires pointed out that Senator France’s proposal was conditional on not conflicting with the Washington Naval Treaty. As Article XVIII of the Treaty expressly prohibited the donation, sale, or any other form of transfer to third countries of warships covered by the agreement, the proposal had no prospect of approval on legal grounds.
  • There is some ambiguity in the reports, and it isn’t clear whether the vessels proposed for transfer were Connecticut class, Rhode Island class, or potentially some mix of the two.

Reference: Bartelski, AS & Bartelski, JA, 2009, Okręty, które nie podniosły biało-czerwonej bandery (3): „Polska eskadra pancerników”, Morze, Statki i Okręty, 10/89, pp18-21.
