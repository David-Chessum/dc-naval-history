Sale of CMB 123 to the Netherlands

CMB 123 was one of the 40’ CMBs built for the Royal Navy during World War I, by Thornycroft at Hampton as hull number 1691. She was delivered to the RN on 14 May 1920, and subsequently paid off into reserve at Portsmouth on 21 September 1923. On 30 October 1923 she was allocated to Hood for the 1924 Special Service Squadron World Cruise, and she was transferred to Thornycroft’s yard to be fitted with a V-12 engine. However there was insufficient time to complete the upgrade, and she was exchanged with CMB 43 (which had been sold after WW1, and was now owned by Thornycroft). CMB 43 (renamed CMB 123) was delivered to the Admiralty and deployed with Hood.

The original CMB 123 was retained by Thornycroft, but her ultimate fate is unclear. There is a report that she was sold to the Royal Netherlands Navy in 1926 (one reference states 1928) however lists of international sales show no sales of a 40’ boat to the Dutch, and no record has been identified of such a vessel in Dutch service.
